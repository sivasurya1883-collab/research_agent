Imagine our fictional insurance brokerage has acquired another smaller brokerage called Lakeshore Insurance.

Lakeshore comes with its own applications, bank feeds, external carrier portals, files, support owners and processes.

Before connecting Lakeshore into our enterprise, we need answers to three fundamental questions:

1. What did we acquire?
 What applications, feeds, external dependencies and business processes does Lakeshore have?

2. Is it ready to integrate?
 Does it meet our enterprise standards? Are ownership, monitoring, dependencies and business processes defined?

3. Once integrated, is the business actually operating correctly?
 We don't just want to know that servers and applications are UP. We need to know whether critical business journeys are working.

That's what we're building.

Part 2: What "Apply Cash" means

This is probably the most important domain concept.

"Receiving money and knowing what the money belongs to are two different things."

Imagine:

Plain Text
Received: $10,000

The brokerage needs to understand:

Plain Text
Who paid this?
 
Which customer/account?
 
What does this payment relate to?
 
How much should be applied?

If everything matches:

Plain Text
Received $10,000
Applied $10,000
 
Unapplied $0

Good.

But suppose the information required to identify the payment isn't understood:

Plain Text
Received $10,000
Applied $6,000
 
Unapplied $4,000

That remaining $4,000 represents unapplied cash in our synthetic scenario.

The business now has a problem even though its servers might be perfectly healthy.

Part 12: What the Copilot says

Executive enters:

What should I be worried about right now?

The Copilot could respond:

Lakeshore's Cash-to-Carrier journey requires attention.

An incoming payment file failed the expected format check. Payment matching has subsequently deteriorated and the cash-application backlog is increasing.

Business risk: The current processing cycle may miss its expected deadline.

Probable cause: Incoming file structure differs from the expected structure.

Recommended action: Review the changed field mapping, correct the mapping and reprocess the affected file.

Confidence: High

Evidence: file-validation failure, reduced matching rate and increased processing backlog.

The challenge specifically calls for evidence-backed probable cause, business impact, confidence, persona-specific explanation and recommended actions.
